What marketing automation actually does

Every automation has two parts: a trigger (something that happens) and one or more actions (what the system does next). A form submission, an incoming call, a link click, a tag being added or a customer's birthday can all be triggers. Actions include sending a text or email, waiting a set time, moving a deal to a new pipeline stage or notifying a team member.

For a small business, the value is consistency. The follow-up you mean to do when things are quiet happens every time, including on your busiest days.

What to look for in marketing automation software

  1. It lives in your CRM. Automations should read and update the same contact record your team uses, so a customer who replies stops getting the sequence.
  2. It covers text and email. Many local customers respond to texts first. Look for two-way SMS alongside email.
  3. Triggers from every channel. Forms, calls, link clicks, bookings and pipeline changes should all be able to start an automation. See automation triggers.
  4. A visual builder. You should be able to build if/then logic and delays without code. See workflow automations.
  5. Templates you can reuse. Save your best messages so every response is consistent. See SMS and email templates.
  6. Reporting. You need to see which lead sources and campaigns turn into booked work. See reporting and analytics.
