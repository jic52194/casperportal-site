Mastering Local SEO for Georgetown Businesses

Dominate your local market by understanding how search engines rank local businesses. Learn the framework to get discovered by high-intent customers in Georgetown and the North Shore.

For: Local business owners in Georgetown, MA and the North Shore who want to rank in Google's Map Pack when customers search '[service] near me' or '[service] Georgetown MA'.

By Jared Casper · March 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Why This Matters

Your business exists. Google doesn't know it's the best option. Right now, someone in Georgetown is searching 'plumber Georgetown MA' and calling whoever appears at the top — whether that's you or a competitor.

What Local SEO Is — and Why Georgetown Businesses Can Win It

When someone types "HVAC Georgetown MA" or "best salon near me" into Google, three businesses appear at the top in the Map Pack. The businesses below them are essentially invisible — 70% of users click one of those three results and never scroll further (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey, 2024).

Local SEO is the process of getting your business into those top three spots. It's not the same as regular SEO — it's specifically about signals that tell Google your business is real, active, local, and trusted by actual customers.

The good news: most Georgetown and North Shore businesses are not optimizing for this at all. The barrier to ranking is lower than you think if you're consistent.

Step 1: Optimize Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single highest-leverage thing you can do for local SEO. It's what drives your appearance in the Map Pack.

The basics that most businesses skip: - Business category: Choose the most specific primary category possible. "Plumber" outperforms "Home Services" every time. - Business hours: Keep them accurate and updated. Google factors responsiveness. - Photos: Profiles with 100+ photos get significantly more calls than those with under 10. Add photos of your team, your work, your location, and your vehicles. - Services section: List every service you offer with keyword-rich descriptions. "Emergency drain cleaning in Georgetown MA" beats "Drain services." - Posts: Google rewards active profiles. Post weekly — a special offer, a completed project, a tip for homeowners. - Q&A: Seed your own Q&A section with common questions your customers ask.

Georgetown-specific tip: In your GBP description, mention Georgetown, Rowley, Boxford, Groveland, and the surrounding towns you serve. Google uses this to understand your service radius.

Step 2: Fix Your NAP Consistency

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone. Google cross-references your business information across the entire web. If your name is "Georgetown Electric" on Google but "Georgetown Electric Co." on Yelp and "Georgetown Electrical Services" on your website — Google loses confidence in your listing and drops your rank.

Where to check and fix your NAP: - Google Business Profile - Your website header and contact page - Yelp - Apple Maps / Bing Places - Facebook Business Page - Better Business Bureau - Yellow Pages - Chamber of Commerce listing

Use the exact same format everywhere. If you abbreviate "Street" as "St." on Google, abbreviate it everywhere. If your phone number is (978) 555-1234, don't list it as 978-555-1234 on other directories. The consistency is what matters.

This takes about 2 hours to audit and fix once. Most businesses never do it.

Step 3: Build Review Velocity (Not Just Review Count)

Google doesn't just look at how many reviews you have — it looks at how frequently you're getting new ones. A business with 40 reviews all received in 2022 ranks lower than a business with 35 reviews that gets 3–5 new reviews per month.

How to build review velocity: 1. Ask every customer for a review — in person, immediately after the job 2. Send an automated review request via SMS 24 hours after job completion 3. Include a direct Google review link (not your homepage) 4. Respond to every review — positive and negative — within 48 hours

Georgetown example: A Georgetown landscaping company went from 12 Google reviews to 67 in 4 months by adding an automated post-job SMS that sent a review link directly to the customer's phone. Their Map Pack ranking moved from position 6 to position 2 for "landscaper Georgetown MA" over the same period.

CasperPortal automates this entire process — review requests fire automatically after a job is marked complete.

Step 4: Build Local Citations

A citation is any mention of your business name, address, and phone number on another website. The more quality citations you have (especially on authoritative directories), the more Google trusts your business is real and local.

Priority citation sources for North Shore businesses: - Yelp - Apple Maps - Bing Places - Yellow Pages - Angi (formerly Angie's List) - HomeAdvisor - Better Business Bureau (BBB) - Essex County Chamber of Commerce - Massachusetts Business listings - Local newspaper directories (The Daily News, Haverhill Gazette)

Aim for 20–40 consistent citations as a foundation. Don't pay for spammy citation blasts — quality and consistency matter more than volume.

Step 5: Target Local Keywords on Your Website

Your GBP alone isn't enough. Your website needs to reinforce your location signals.

Local keyword patterns to target: - "[Service] Georgetown MA" (e.g., "plumber Georgetown MA") - "[Service] near me" (Google fills in the location) - "[Service] Essex County" - "[Service] North Shore MA"

Where to use them: - Your homepage H1 and meta title - Your service pages (one page per core service) - Your footer (include your full address and NAP) - Your blog (write locally-specific content)

Example: Instead of a generic "Services" page, create a page titled "Plumbing Services in Georgetown, MA — Emergency & Scheduled Repairs" with a unique 500-word description of what you do in Georgetown specifically.

If you serve multiple towns — Georgetown, Rowley, Ipswich, Newburyport — create individual location pages for each. Don't use the same content on every page. Google penalizes duplicate location pages.

Here is a tip: Local SEO is not a one-time project. It's a monthly discipline. The businesses that win locally are the ones that show up consistently.
