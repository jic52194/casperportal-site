Local SEO for Small Businesses: A 5-Step Guide for Georgetown and the North Shore

By Jared Casper, Founder · March 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Local SEO for small businesses is the work of showing Google that your business is real, nearby and trusted, so you appear in the map results when people search for your service in your town. It comes down to five things: a complete Google Business Profile, consistent contact details, a steady flow of reviews, local citations and location-focused website pages.

What local SEO is — and why small businesses can win it

When someone types "HVAC Georgetown MA" or "salon near me" into Google, a small set of businesses appears in the map results above the regular listings. Those are the businesses that get the calls. Local SEO is the process of earning a place there.

It's different from general SEO. It's specifically about signals that tell Google your business is real, active, local and trusted by actual customers. Many small businesses on the North Shore don't work on these signals at all, which means consistent effort can go a long way.

Step 1: Complete and maintain your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is what drives your appearance in the map results, so it's the first place to put your effort.

  • Primary category: choose the most specific one that fits. "Plumber" is clearer to Google than "Home Services."
  • Hours: keep them accurate, including holidays.
  • Photos: add real photos of your team, your work, your location and your vehicles, and keep adding new ones.
  • Services: list every service you offer with a plain description. "Emergency drain cleaning in Georgetown, MA" says more than "Drain services."
  • Posts: share updates regularly — a seasonal offer, a finished project, a tip for customers.
  • Questions: answer the questions customers ask most often, right on your profile.

Georgetown tip: in your profile description, name Georgetown and the nearby towns you actually serve — Rowley, Boxford, Groveland and so on — so Google understands your service area.

Step 2: Make your NAP consistent everywhere

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone. Google compares your business details across the web. If you're "Georgetown Electric" on Google, "Georgetown Electric Co." on Yelp and "Georgetown Electrical Services" on your website, Google has less confidence that they're all the same business.

Where to check:

  • Google Business Profile
  • Your website header, footer and contact page
  • Yelp
  • Apple Maps and Bing Places
  • Your Facebook business page
  • Better Business Bureau and Yellow Pages
  • Your local chamber of commerce listing

Use exactly the same format everywhere. If you write "St." on Google, write "St." everywhere. Pick one phone number format and stick to it. Consistency is the whole point.

Step 3: Build a steady flow of reviews

Review count matters, but so does recency. A profile that keeps getting new reviews looks active to customers and to Google; a profile whose newest review is two years old does not.

How to keep reviews coming in:

  1. Ask every customer, in person, right after the job.
  2. Follow up with an automatic text that includes your direct Google review link (not your homepage).
  3. Send one gentle reminder to customers who haven't left a review yet.
  4. Reply to every review, positive and negative, promptly and professionally.

Step 4: Build local citations

A citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number on another website. Accurate citations on trusted directories help confirm that your business is real and local.

Citation sources worth claiming for North Shore businesses:

  • Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places
  • Yellow Pages and the Better Business Bureau
  • Industry directories (for example, Angi or HomeAdvisor for home services)
  • Your local chamber of commerce and Massachusetts business listings
  • Local newspaper and community directories

Focus on accurate listings on directories people actually use. Avoid paid "citation blasts" to low-quality sites — consistency matters more than volume.

Step 5: Target local keywords on your website

Your Google profile alone isn't enough. Your website should reinforce where you work.

Local keyword patterns:

  • "[Service] Georgetown MA" (for example, "plumber Georgetown MA")
  • "[Service] near me" (Google fills in the location)
  • "[Service] Essex County" or "[Service] North Shore MA"

Where to use them: your homepage heading and title, one page per core service, your footer (with your full NAP) and articles about local topics.

Example: instead of a generic "Services" page, create "Plumbing Services in Georgetown, MA — Emergency and Scheduled Repairs" with a description written specifically about the work you do there. If you serve several towns, give each one its own page with genuinely different content; near-identical copies of the same page rarely help. You can build these pages with the landing page builder.

Local SEO isn't a one-time project. It's a monthly habit — and the businesses that show up consistently are the ones that get found.

Frequently asked questions

What is local SEO for small businesses?

Local SEO is the work of helping your business appear in Google's map and local results when nearby customers search for your service. It relies on your Google Business Profile, consistent contact details, reviews, citations and location-specific website pages.

What is NAP consistency?

NAP stands for Name, Address and Phone number. NAP consistency means those details are written exactly the same way on your Google profile, your website and every directory where your business is listed.

Do Google reviews affect local rankings?

Reviews are one of the signals of a trusted, active local business, and a steady flow of recent reviews also helps customers choose you. Ask every customer, make it easy with a direct review link, and reply to every review.

Should I create a separate page for every town I serve?

Yes, if you genuinely serve those towns — but give each page unique, useful content about your work there. Near-identical pages with only the town name changed rarely help.
