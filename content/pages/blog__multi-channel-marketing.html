Multi-Channel Marketing for Local Businesses: The 3-Channel System

By Jared Casper, Founder · March 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Multi-channel marketing for local businesses means reaching customers through several connected channels instead of relying on one. A practical system uses three: your Google Business Profile to get found, SMS to follow up and re-engage quickly, and email to nurture people who aren't ready yet — with each channel handing customers to the next.

The single-channel trap

Most North Shore small businesses rely on one main lead source:

  • Referrals — great when business is good, worrying when they slow down
  • Paid ads — work until the budget runs out or a competitor outbids you
  • Social media — reach can change overnight when the platform changes
  • Word of mouth — hard to predict and hard to grow on purpose

When that one channel stalls, your whole pipeline stalls. You don't need ten channels. You need three that work together.

Channel 1: Google Business Profile (free, high-intent)

Your Google Business Profile is free and puts you in front of people who are searching for your service right now — "contractor Newburyport MA" or "salon near me." That makes it the first channel to get right, not an afterthought.

What to do every week:

  • Post a photo of recent work (before and after, your team on site, a finished project).
  • Reply to new reviews.
  • Add new services or seasonal offers to your listing.
  • Check and answer questions on your profile.

Channel 2: SMS follow-up (the fast channel)

Text messages are personal and quick to read, which makes SMS the fastest way to re-engage a lead who went quiet, remind a customer about an appointment, or win back someone who hasn't booked in a while.

Three SMS automations to start with:

  1. Missed-call text-back — an automatic text when a call goes unanswered.
  2. Appointment reminders — automatic texts the day before and a few hours before each appointment.
  3. Win-back campaign — a text to customers who haven't booked in a few months.

Example win-back message: "Hi [First Name], it's been a while. We're running a [seasonal offer] this month — want to get back on the schedule? Reply here or book at [link]."

Channel 3: Email nurture (the long game)

Email isn't dead for local businesses — it's underused. Many either don't email their list at all or only send promotions with no plan behind them.

A good nurture system does two things:

  1. Keeps you top of mind for people who aren't ready to buy yet.
  2. Converts warm leads who need a few more touchpoints before booking.

A simple local email calendar:

  • Monthly: "What's new at [Business Name]" — updates, seasonal services, new team members.
  • Quarterly: a helpful tip or short guide related to your service.
  • As needed: a time-sensitive offer with a clear deadline.

What to avoid: blasting discounts with no context. Give people a reason to stay subscribed — tips, education, and what you're doing in the community.

How the three channels work together

Each channel covers a different stage of the customer journey:

  • Google Business Profile brings in new customers who are searching right now.
  • SMS follows up and re-engages leads quickly.
  • Email nurtures people who aren't ready yet and keeps past customers coming back.

The loop looks like this:

  1. A new customer finds you on Google.
  2. They call or fill out a form.
  3. If they don't book right away, an SMS follow-up sequence starts.
  4. They book; confirmations and reminders go out automatically.
  5. After the job, they get an automatic review request.
  6. They join your email list and get your monthly email.
  7. A few months later, a win-back text invites them back.

Each channel feeds the next, and the loop keeps running whether or not you're watching it.

Frequently asked questions

What is multi-channel marketing for a local business?

It means reaching customers through more than one connected channel — for example Google Business Profile, text messages and email — so you aren't dependent on a single lead source like referrals or ads.

Which marketing channels should a local business start with?

Start with your Google Business Profile because it's free and reaches people actively searching. Add SMS for fast follow-up and reminders, then email to nurture leads and past customers over time.

What is a win-back campaign?

A win-back campaign is a message sent to past customers who haven't booked in a while, usually with a reason to come back such as a seasonal service or offer. It's one of the simplest ways to get work from people who already know you.
