Reputation Management: How to Get 50+ Google Reviews This Year

Reviews are the most powerful trust signal for local businesses. Learn the exact framework for building a 5-star reputation in Essex County without manual effort.

For: Business owners who know they need more reviews but find the manual process of asking, following up, and tracking them impossible to manage.

By Jared Casper · April 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Why This Matters

You have 100s of happy customers but only 12 reviews. Your competitor has 80 reviews and is taking your leads, even if they aren't as good as you.

Your Reviews Are Selling for You (Or Against You)

98% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses (BrightLocal, 2024). In a town like Georgetown or Haverhill, your reputation isn't just word-of-mouth anymore — it's digital.

When someone searches for your service, they make a split-second decision based on two numbers: your star rating and your review count. If you have a 4.8 with 12 reviews and your competitor has a 4.6 with 150 reviews, they win. Every time.

Google Cares About 'Freshness' (Review Velocity)

Most business owners think they just need "a lot" of reviews. Google's algorithm is smarter. It looks at Review Velocity — how many new reviews you're getting consistently.

10 reviews in the last month is worth more for your ranking than 100 reviews from three years ago. This is why you need a system that asks for reviews every single week, not a one-time "push" where you email your whole list once a year.

The Automated Ask: SMS vs. Email

If you email a review link, it has a 20% open rate and a 2% click rate. If you text a review link, it has a 98% open rate and a 15-20% click rate.

The CasperPortal Workflow↗ : 1. Job is marked 'Complete' in your pipeline↗ 2. Wait 2 hours (while the customer is still happy with the result) 3. Send SMS: *"Hey [First Name], thanks for choosing us! Would you mind leaving us a quick review? It helps us out a ton: [Link]"* 4. If no review after 3 days → Send one gentle reminder SMS.

This system alone typically increases review volume by 300-500% within the first 90 days.

The 'Feedback Loop': Stopping Negative Reviews Before They Happen

One of the biggest fears of asking for reviews is getting a 1-star rating in public. Smart reputation management uses a "feedback loop."

Instead of sending every customer straight to Google, you can ask for a rating first (1-5 stars). If they pick 4 or 5, send them to Google. If they pick 1, 2, or 3, send them to a private feedback form so you can fix the issue before it becomes a public stain on your reputation.

Responding to Reviews: The SEO Multiplier

Google explicitly states that responding to reviews improves your local SEO. It shows you're an active, engaged business. But more importantly, it shows *future* customers that you care.

Pro Tip: When you respond, use your local keywords. *"Thanks for the review, Sarah! We love being the top-rated plumber in Georgetown MA."* This helps Google connect your business to those specific search terms.

Here is a tip: A business that responds to 100% of reviews ranks significantly higher than one that ignores them.
